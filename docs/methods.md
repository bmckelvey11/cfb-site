# Methods

How the repo's numbers are computed: the formula, the values plugged in, the assumption each rests on, and the alternative that was rejected. Vocabulary stays in [`CONTEXT.md`](../CONTEXT.md); this file is where a term's `method:` pointer lands. Which of these numbers a result is *required* to report, and what invalidates it, is [`model-evaluation-standard.md`](model-evaluation-standard.md). Each entry names the script that implements it. Same rule as the glossary: edit an entry in place rather than adding a second one.

## Prices and edge

- **Break-even rate** — the win rate at which a flat bettor nets zero at a price: `p_be = 1 / (1 + b)`, where `b` is the decimal payout per unit risked (`100/|A|` for a negative American price `A`, `A/100` for a positive one). At −110, `b = 0.909` and `p_be = 52.38%`; at −120, 54.55%; at −102, 50.5%. Edge is win rate minus this, at the price actually taken, never at −110 by default. (`breakeven` in `research/bankroll/scripts/mc_combined_totals.py`)
- **Pricing law** — PFF's under probability re-evaluated at any line: normal CDF of (line − projection) over a sigma that grows with the total. Used to reprice a flag at a book's number. One point of total is worth roughly four points of win probability at a 50s total. (`research/totals/scripts/match_greenline_books.py`)

## Scoring a forecast

- **Brier score** — mean squared error of a probability: `(1/n)·Σ(p_i − y_i)²`, with `p_i` the forecast and `y_i` the 0/1 outcome. Strictly proper, so its expectation is minimised by reporting the true probability. Interpretable (a squared error in probability units) and stable at extreme forecasts, which is exactly why it under-punishes confident mistakes — report it beside log loss, never alone. Three-way outcomes take RPS, a full predictive distribution takes CRPS. (`docs/model-evaluation-standard.md`)
- **Log loss** — `−(1/n)·Σ[y_i·log(p_i) + (1−y_i)·log(1−p_i)]`. Also strictly proper, and unbounded as a confident forecast goes wrong, so it is the warning against overconfidence that Brier does not give: a model that never says 0.02 cannot be caught by Brier alone. Undefined at p = 0 or 1, so production forecasts are clipped and the clip is stated, not assumed.
- **Brier skill score** — `1 − Brier_model / Brier_reference`. Above 0 beats the reference, 1 is perfect, negative is worse. A raw Brier is not comparable across markets with different base rates, so every score is reported against three references: the base rate, the de-vigged market price **at the same timestamp**, and the incumbent production model. The market comparison is the one that decides. Scoring a Wednesday model against Saturday's close mixes forecasting skill with information that arrived later.
- **Brier decomposition** — `Brier = Reliability − Resolution + Uncertainty`. Reliability is calibration error (lower better), resolution is the ability to separate games into genuinely different risk groups (higher better), uncertainty is the base-rate difficulty of the sample and is not the model's doing. Two models tie on Brier for opposite reasons — one calibrated but timid, one sharp but overconfident — so a Brier reported without the split hides which one you have. (`docs/model-evaluation-standard.md`)
- **Calibration slope and intercept** — fit `logit(y) ~ a + b·logit(p)` out of sample; ideal is `a = 0`, `b = 1`. A nonzero intercept means the model over- or under-predicts on average; a slope below 1 means its probabilities are too extreme, the standard overfitting signature and the one that costs most, because the most confident bets are the largest. Report both by edge band, odds band, season and book — global calibration hides local damage. Fixed-bin ECE is a supporting picture only: it moves with the bin edges and can rank two models wrongly, so it never replaces a proper score. A constant forecast is perfectly calibrated and useless, which is why calibration and resolution are scored apart.

## Market-relative value

- **No-vig (de-vigged) price** — the market's implied probability with the bookmaker's margin removed, so it can be set against a model probability. Two-sided normalisation `p_fair = p_i / (p_home + p_away)` is the default; shin, power and logarithmic de-vigging shift favourite–longshot pricing and are a stated sensitivity, not an afterthought. Raw implied probabilities sum above 1, which makes every bet look bad by the size of the hold.
- **CLV, measured** — closing-line value against a **predeclared** reference: named book or consensus, exact cutoff, de-vig method, push handling, and the rule for stale or missing quotes, all fixed before the sample is scored. Three forms, all reported: probability CLV `p_close,fair − 1/o_bet`, price-ratio CLV `o_bet / o_close,fair − 1`, and expected return at close `p_close,fair·o_bet − 1`, plus the beat-close rate and a clustered interval. There is no universal closing line; picking the most favourable book after the fact inflates CLV without anyone lying. (`docs/model-evaluation-standard.md`)
- **Market-conditioned incremental test** — the question CLV cannot answer: does the model know anything the market does not? Out of sample, fit `logit P(Y=1) = α + β·logit(p_market) + γ·z_model`, where `z_model` is the model's log-odds difference from the market. A stable, correctly signed `γ` — with an improved proper score and calibration — is evidence of incremental information; `γ ≈ 0` means the model is echoing the price back at you. This is a forecast-encompassing test: it asks whether one forecast explains variation the other cannot.
- **Edge-decile monotonicity** — sort bets into ten pre-declared bins by the model's own claimed edge and check that realised return rises across them. If the 8% edges do not beat the 2% edges, the probability scale is wrong even when aggregate ROI is positive, and the betting threshold sitting on that scale is unsupported. The repo has already seen this fail: Greenline's stated edge does not order outcomes. (`research/totals/docs/greenline-edge-cap-revisit-2026-09-17.md`)
- **Turnover ROI** — `Σ net profit / Σ stake`, the purity-of-price-edge number, reported **net** of commission, pushes, slippage, rejected fills and any cost that scales with betting, and always beside turnover, net profit, independent-event count and an interval. Distinct from bankroll return (ending/starting − 1), which is staking- and path-dependent, and from log growth. A naked ROI percentage states neither scale nor uncertainty and is not a result.
- **Profit concentration** — how much of the total came from how few bets: median and trimmed return, share of profit from the top 1/5/10 wagers, ROI after dropping the best event, week and season, and the spread across books and bet types. Not disqualifying on its own — betting returns are discrete and heavy-tailed — but a headline ROI carried by three longshots has the evidentiary weight of three observations, not of the bet count.

## Records and their intervals

- **Wilson interval** — the 95% score interval on a win rate: centre `(p̂ + z²/2n) / (1 + z²/n)`, half-width `z·√(p̂(1−p̂)/n + z²/4n²) / (1 + z²/n)`, z = 1.96 (1.645 for the 90% version). Used instead of the normal interval because it stays inside [0, 1] and does not collapse on a 0–n or n–0 record. The **floor** is its lower end and is a frequentist bound, not a posterior quantile; "bet at the floor" means taking the floor as the true rate and asking whether it still beats break-even. Games in one week share weather and slate shocks, so intervals are slightly narrow. (`wilson` in `research/totals/scripts/greenline_season_review.py`)
- **MDE** — minimum detectable win rate: the smallest true rate a sample of that size could distinguish from break-even (one-sided, α 0.05, power 0.8). Stated with every record. A split whose observed rate sits under its own MDE is **below floor**: not evidence either way. (`research/totals/scripts/greenline_season_review.py`)
- **Rule of three** — when an event is seen 0 times in n trials the 95% upper bound on its rate is about `3 / n`. "0 busts in 50,000 paths" therefore means "under 0.006% under the model", not zero, and busts are reported as counts for this reason. Near a cap the binomial standard error `√(p(1−p)/n)` says how far a row is from the line: at P(−25%) = 1% on 50,000 paths that is 0.045 points, so 0.86% and 1.27% are not ties. (`research/bankroll/docs/bankroll-stress-2026-09-17.md`)

## Priors

- **Jeffreys prior** — `Beta(½, ½)`, so a record of w–l gives the posterior `Beta(w + ½, l + ½)`. Every simulated path draws its own win rate from this posterior once and keeps it for the season: that draw is the **parameter uncertainty**, and it is why more bets cannot remove all risk. The floor on P(losing money) is P(p < break-even) under the posterior, 15% for Greenline under the planning prior. Uniform `Beta(1, 1)` was not used; Jeffreys is invariant to reparameterising p and adds half a game to each side, not a full one. (`rng.beta(... + 0.5, ... + 0.5)` in `mc_combined_totals.py`)
- **Prior** — the graded record a leg's win rate is drawn from, as a Beta. The Greenline **planning prior** is the discounted-history posterior `Beta(27 + κ·114 + ½, 22 + κ·87 + ½)` at κ = 0.5: the 2026 flags (27–22) at full weight, the 2023–25 history unders (114–87) at half, Jeffreys ½ on each side. Mean 56.1%, P(p below 52.38%) = 15%. κ = 0 is `n49` (2026 only, mean 55.0%), κ = 1 is `pooled` (mean 56.4%); those two are the **bracket**, reported alongside and never the planning number. κ is a judgement, not an estimate: a hierarchical fit with a group effect for source is the deferred alternative. (`planning_p_gl` in `mc_combined_totals.py`; `research/bankroll/docs/bankroll-stress-2026-09-17.md`)
- **Haircut** — the over-zero selection correction: the walk-forward record is 151–83, the planning rate is its Wilson lower bound 58.2%, and the projection subtracts a further 0.063 because the guide's live picks are a selected subset. The stress harness can centre it elsewhere (`oz_center`) or make it uncertain per path (`oz_extra_sd`). (`OZ_SELECTION_HAIRCUT` in `mc_combined_totals.py`)

## Testing a split or a filter

- **Split tests** — a k-way split (bands, edge buckets) is tested with a chi-square of independence on the k×2 win/loss table; one cell against the rest with a two-sided Fisher exact test, which is exact at any cell size. Neither knows the split was chosen after looking. (`research/totals/scripts/band_significance.py`)
- **Holm correction** — for m pre-registered tests, sort the p-values ascending and compare the i-th to `α / (m − i + 1)`, stopping at the first failure. Controls the family-wise error rate across the six situational filters at 5% without Bonferroni's full penalty. The big-favorite filter's Holm p is 0.256. (`research/totals/docs/greenline-under-filters-2026-09-17.md`)
- **Selection-on-selection** — a split scored on the same sample that chose it (the 55–59.5 band was picked from the 2023–25 history, then reported pooled with it). Such a record is not evidence. The honest tests are a best-of-k correction (how often the strongest of k inspected splits looks this strong under one common rate) and an out-of-sample ordering test (score new flags by the old split's rate, report AUC). (`research/totals/scripts/band_significance.py`)
- **Best-of-k correction** — the p-value for "the strongest of k inspected splits looks this strong": simulate the k cells at one common win rate with their observed sizes over many seasons, and count how often the best cell reaches the observed best rate (and the worst cell the observed worst). For six bands the best-cell p is 0.82; the raw Fisher p on the same cell is 0.11. It still understates the selection when the cell boundaries were drawn by hand. (`best_of_k` in `band_significance.py`)
- **Ordering test (AUC)** — the out-of-sample check on a ranking: score each new case by the old sample's rate for its cell, then the Mann-Whitney statistic between winners' and losers' scores divided by `n_win·n_loss` is the AUC. 0.5 is no information. The band ranking from 2023–25 scored 0.47 on 2026 flags. About 150 cases are needed to detect AUC 0.60 at 5%. (`oos_ordering` in `band_significance.py`)
- **Filter regression** — logistic `win ~ filter + source + filter×source`, standard errors clustered by calendar day. `b_filter` is the filter's log-odds shift in the history stratum, `b_inter` how much that shift differs in 2026. A large, opposite-signed interaction means the two strata are different populations, not that the filter works. `sep` marks a cell with no losses, where the interaction is unidentified and the fit drops to `filter + source`. (`research/totals/docs/greenline-under-filters-2026-09-17.md`)

- **Clustered and block bootstrap** — bets on the same game, slate, week or information shock are not independent, and a row-wise bootstrap destroys exactly the structure that makes an interval honest. Resample whole clusters (event at minimum; day or week blocks for serial dependence, two-way when game and time effects both bite) so the dependence survives into the interval. This is the repair for the narrowness the Wilson entry above only flags. (`docs/model-evaluation-standard.md`)
- **Paired score test** — compare two models on the identical observations, take the per-observation difference in proper score, and test it with a dependence-robust Diebold–Mariano statistic. Pairing is the point: the difficulty of the games cancels, so the comparison is about the forecasts and not about which sample drew easier weeks. Report the effect size with the p-value — a detectable advantage can still be too small to survive the hold.
- **DSR and PBO** — the two corrections for having searched. Deflated Sharpe Ratio discounts a Sharpe for the number of trials attempted and for non-normal returns; Probability of Backtest Overfitting estimates how often the in-sample winner lands below the out-of-sample median. Both need an honest trial count, which is why the trial ledger is a recording obligation and not a formality: the winner's ordinary p-value, ROI and Sharpe are optimistic by an amount only the search history can price. Holm (above) controls the family-wise error of a *pre-registered* set; DSR/PBO price an *exploratory search*. Not substitutes. (`docs/model-evaluation-standard.md`)

## Scoring distribution

- **Score-delta attribution** — points on a play are `tot - lag(tot)` where `tot = offenseScore + defenseScore`, ordered within a game by `period, driveNumber, playNumber`, clamped to 1–8. The score fields are post-play, so the delta lands on the scoring play itself and a touchdown carries its extra point in the same bucket; `playNumber` alone is not a game-wide order (it restarts per drive) and ordering by it puts 43% of deltas negative. About 2.4% of plays flagged `scoring` produce a non-positive or out-of-range delta and are dropped. The 2015–2025 total is 56.50 points per game over 12,007 games. (`scripts/analyze_scoring_by_minute.py`)
- **Game-minute cell** — the denominator for a conditional scoring rate: one row per (game, quarter, minute) with `minute = 15 - clock_minutes`, generated as a full 60-cell grid per game rather than from the plays, so minutes with no snap count as zero-point exposure. Each cell is bucketed once, by the state at its first play, carried forward from the last observed play where the minute has none. About 34% of cells contain no snap; counting only cells that do inflates every rate by roughly 1.52× (0.942 → 1.434) and lets a clock-killing leader look like a fast scorer, because leading reduces snaps per minute. Per-play rates were the rejected alternative: well defined, but they discard exactly the play-count effect the question is about. (`scripts/analyze_scoring_by_minute_splits.py`)
- **Share of own points** — a bucket's quarter or half share is `sum(points in Qk) / sum(all its points)`, a ratio of pooled totals. The mean of per-game shares was rejected: a team held scoreless has no defined share, and it would weight a 3-point game as heavily as a 45-point one. Pooled across 2015–2025, every team splits 22.28 / 29.77 / 22.63 / 25.32 percent across Q1–Q4, first half 52.06%. (`scripts/analyze_scoring_by_team_type.py`)
- **Neighbour lift** — an exact value `v`'s count over the mean of its four same-parity neighbours `v±2, v±4`. Same parity cancels the odd/even effect; because the window is symmetric about `v`, a least-squares line through those four points evaluated at `v` is just their mean, so any *linear* slope in the envelope cancels exactly. Only local curvature leaks through, and near the peak it flatters the lift — hence the second column, the same ratio against a quadratic fitted through six same-parity neighbours. Restricted to bins with at least 100 games so the ranking is not Poisson noise. No multiple-comparison correction across roughly 70 candidate bins, so a lone lift in the 1.4–1.5× band is descriptive. (`scripts/analyze_total_points_distribution.py`)
- **Prior-season bucketing** — team-type labels join on `season - 1`: SP+ offence from `stg.ratings` by `teamId`, tempo as `stg.advanced_season_stats.offense_plays` over that season's games, each cut into quartiles *per season* so a bucket means the same thing across the scoring drift. Coverage is 127–136 of the 128–136 teams a season; teams with no prior FBS season are dropped. Same-season labels were rejected: tempo and SP+ offence are aggregates of the very games being bucketed, so a "high offence" label is built partly from the fourth-quarter points it is then used to explain. The price is a stale label, which biases every measured effect toward zero. Buckets remain confounded with game total and with opponent quality; neither is controlled. (`scripts/analyze_scoring_by_team_type.py`)

## Bankroll projection

- **Same-week shock** — one latent scoring-environment draw `Z_w ~ N(0,1)` per week, shared by every bet that Saturday through a Gaussian copula: bet i's latent is `√ρ·Z_w + √(1−ρ)·ε_i`, and it wins when the latent's normal CDF falls below its win rate `p`. ρ = 0.10 on the latent scale gives outcome (win/loss) correlation ≈ 0.063 at p ≈ 0.56; the two are not the same number and the sweep reports both. Over-zero OVERs take `+Z_w`, Greenline UNDERs take `−Z_w`, so the legs hedge each other's scoring risk. Given the shock the week's wins are a conditional binomial, which is why the variance of a week exceeds the independent binomial by roughly `1 + (n−1)·0.063`. The shock has one sign; a model-or-market failure that hurts both legs together is not modelled and is the deferred second factor. (`research/bankroll/docs/mc-method-2026-09-17.md`)
- **Stress scenario** — a projection run with one or more skeptical knobs on: `oz_center`, `oz_extra_sd`, `gl_kappa` (0 = 2026 only, 1 = fully pooled), `gl_marginal_penalty` (unders beyond six a week win at p − d), `rho`. Twenty-six scenarios; the decision is the unit that passes the cap across them, not any one scenario as the true model. (`research/bankroll/scripts/bankroll_stress.py`)
- **Path measures** — max drawdown from the running peak, weeks ending below the start, and ES5 (mean of the worst 5% of endings). Reported because the fan chart of endings hides what a season feels like on the way. (`research/bankroll/docs/bankroll-stress-2026-09-17.md`)
- **VaR and Expected Shortfall** — VaR at level α is the loss the bankroll exceeds only α of the time; Expected Shortfall is the *mean* loss given that threshold is breached. ES is the one to report: it is coherent (VaR in general is not, and can penalise diversification), and it describes the tail rather than naming its edge. `ES5` in the stress harness is this statistic at α = 5% on season endings; the same number belongs on daily and weekly returns. (`docs/model-evaluation-standard.md`)
- **Downside ratio** — median gain divided by the gap between the median and the 5th percentile. Reported beside the median so a config that buys growth with a fatter left tail is visible. Stake size does not move it; volume does.
- **Drawdown cap** — the per-season constraint on the unit: P(ending the season down 25% or more) ≤ 3% and zero busts, under the planning prior. A constraint, not the objective; the objective is median growth. (`research/bankroll/scripts/bankroll_config_sweep.py`)

## Sizing

- **Kelly fraction** — for a bet paying `b` per unit at win rate `p`, `f* = (p·b − (1−p)) / b` of the bankroll. The numerator is expected profit per unit risked; dividing by `b` converts it to a stake. Negative means no bet. At −110 and p = 56.4%, full Kelly is 8.4%. The simulator's `kelly_unit` takes a fraction (¼) and divides by `1 + (n−1)·ρ_out` for n simultaneous bets, the small-edge approximation to joint Kelly. (`kelly_unit` in `mc_combined_totals.py`)
- **Quarter Kelly** — a quarter of the Kelly fraction, then shrunk for simultaneous bets. Full Kelly maximizes log growth under a tolerance the bankroll does not have; the fraction is the growth-vs-drawdown dial. Off the planning prior with nine simultaneous bets: 1.31%. (`kelly_unit` in `mc_combined_totals.py`)
- **Log growth** — `E[log(final / start)]` over paths, the quantity full Kelly maximises. The growth-optimal Greenline unit under the full simulator is about 4% (pooled prior) or 3% (n49), with P(−25%) of 18–25%; 1% is roughly a quarter of it. Reported beside the median so the price of the drawdown cap is visible. (`research/bankroll/docs/bankroll-stress-2026-09-17.md`)
- **Unit rule** — the Greenline unit is the smaller of quarter Kelly off the planning prior, shrunk for simultaneous bets, and the largest unit that passes the drawdown cap under the planning prior. Quarter Kelly gives 1.31%, the cap gives 1.0%, so the unit is 1%. (`research/bankroll/docs/seed-bankroll-proposal-2026-09-17.md`)
