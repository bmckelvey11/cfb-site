Coverage: 26 linked help articles, including 13 embedded YouTube walkthroughs and 21 article screenshots. Video screenshots below are captured YouTube preview frames. Content reviewed July 16, 2026.

## What this section teaches

Bet Labs helps users turn sports-betting ideas into filter-based historical systems, then checks live games for matches. Good practice repeated across these articles: begin with a reasoned hypothesis, retain enough historical matches, inspect stability across seasons, avoid cherry-picking filters, and verify volatile matches near game time.

### 2. [Understanding 'Money Won' and 'ROI'](https://sportsinsights.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041919872-Understanding-Money-Won-and-ROI)

Money Won uses flat $100 risk per historical match, so $20,214 equals 202.14 units. ROI equals money won divided by total money risked, expressed as percentage; it measures return relative to amount staked rather than raw profit. Both measures recalculate while editing filters, helping show impact of each condition.

### 3. [How to Make 'Fade' Systems](https://sportsinsights.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041803192-How-to-Make-Fade-Systems)

Fade System reverses selected system: instead of betting on teams matching filters, it takes their opponents or opposite side. First build and inspect filters normally, then enable Fade System in upper-right; selecting filters before enabling avoids confusion. Useful where original condition identifies a team to oppose, including performance exceeding Pythagorean expectation.

### 6. [How to Utilize the 'Hide Duplicates' Feature](https://sportsinsights.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040911112-How-to-Utilize-the-Hide-Duplicates-Feature)

Hide Duplicates prevents same game from counting twice when both teams satisfy broad conditions. It is not needed when filters already force one side, such as home/away or favorite/dog. Example: two poor NFL teams playing each other can both meet low win-percentage filter; enabling feature removes those two-sided games from record.

### 7. [Mistakes to Avoid When Creating Bet Labs Systems - Small Sample Size](https://sportsinsights.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041337671-Mistakes-to-Avoid-When-Creating-Bet-Labs-Systems-Small-Sample-Size)

Small samples can show impressive historical cover rate without measuring real repeatable strength. NFL and NCAAF are especially vulnerable because each season adds few games. Systems with only few dozen matches produce rare triggers and can move sharply after small losing streak; favor samples large enough to make 55%-type edge statistically credible.

### 9. [Mistakes to Avoid When Creating Bet Labs Systems - Overfitting](https://sportsinsights.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360041261931-Mistakes-to-Avoid-When-Creating-Bet-Labs-Systems-Overfitting)

Overfitting happens when user assembles many individual values solely because they produced historical profit. Such system can display huge money won and ROI while lacking coherent cause, so future result is unlikely to resemble backtest. System grade helps flag excess individual selections; use fewer, defensible filters and preserve hypothesis before testing.

### 11. [Why do Bet Labs system matches change?](https://sportsinsights.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360035672951-Why-do-Bet-Labs-system-matches-change)

Systems driven by moving spread, betting share, total, or weather can gain or lose matches before game starts. Fixed historical or schedule facts—road team, prior win, division opponent—do not change. Recheck volatile systems near start time before treating shown match as final.

### 12. [What filters are available in Bet Labs?](https://sportsinsights.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360035307572-What-filters-are-available-in-Bet-Labs)

## Practical checklist

1. Start with a testable betting idea, not a profitable-looking slice of history.
2. Use enough matches; treat exceptionally small samples as unproven.
3. Review yearly graph, recent seasons, ROI, and past matches together.
4. Prevent double-counted games with Hide Duplicates where needed.
5. Recheck systems using price, public betting, or weather close to start time.
6. Treat historical results as research, not a guarantee of future performance.
