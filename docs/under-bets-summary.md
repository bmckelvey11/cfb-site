Holds independently in all three seasons (+18.8 / +36.9 / +18.4%). p=0.047.

**Nearly all your under profit comes from the late window.** Early-season unders are a coin flip
paying -110.

You do not appear to be doing this deliberately: your median total is 58.5 in *both* windows, and
your under-share barely moves by month (83–91% all season). The edge falls out of a constant
strategy meeting a seasonal effect — weather, wind, shortened playbooks, defenses catching up to
early-season scheme edges.

The one thing that *does* shift: you bet more sub-50 games late (21.3% vs 7.0% early), which is
mostly the market handing you colder games rather than a change in your selection.

## 5. How the bets are selected (inferred)

Slips carry no reasoning text, so this is inferred from selection profile.

**What it is:** a blanket directional lean that CFB totals are posted too high, applied broadly
across the slate at 2.7 bets/day over 76 game days. 76% take a hook (.5) — you accept the posted
number rather than hunting key numbers. 64% of bets at exactly -110.

**What it is not** — each tested and rejected:

| Kickoff time or day-of-week | Noisy, all cells n<20 except Saturday |

## 6. Statistical honesty

- Late unders (p=0.047) and sub-50 unders (p=0.037) are nominally significant but do **not**
  survive correction for the ~20 hypotheses tested here (Bonferroni alpha ≈0.0025).
- The late-season split's real strength is **replication across three independent seasons**, not
  its p-value. That is the more meaningful evidence.
- 203 bets is thin. Separating a 57% bettor from a 52.4% one with confidence needs ~1,000+.
- No closing-line data in the export → no CLV check possible. CLV would confirm or kill this in
  ~100 bets; win rate needs thousands.
